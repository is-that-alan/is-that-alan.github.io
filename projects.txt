e:T7f5,<h1>Discovering Hidden Sector Relationships Among S&#x26;P 500 Companies</h1>
<p><strong>ISYE 6740 — Computational Data Analytics · Georgia Tech · Fall 2025</strong></p>
<p>A text-analytics and unsupervised-learning study of whether the official GICS sector taxonomy can be reproduced — and challenged — from public text alone. I build text-based representations of all 497 unique S&#x26;P 500 companies from their Wikipedia articles, then test how well interpretable models recover sector structure.</p>
<h2>Key results</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>85.9%</strong> cross-validated accuracy predicting GICS sectors with a Linear SVM on TF-IDF features — competitive with far heavier transformer baselines, while staying fully interpretable.</li>
<li><strong>Spectral clustering</strong> gives the best unsupervised fit (<strong>ARI 0.439, NMI 0.626</strong>), capturing ~<strong>63%</strong> of GICS structure while surfacing cross-sector relationships.</li>
<li>Uniformly low silhouette scores across all cluster counts suggest companies live in a <em>continuous</em> operational space rather than discrete buckets — motivating <strong>alternative factor-based portfolio construction</strong>.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Approach</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Data:</strong> scraped the S&#x26;P 500 constituent table and retrieved full Wikipedia articles via <code>wikipediaapi</code> (502 entries → 497 unique companies after filtering missing articles and duplicate share classes).</li>
<li><strong>Features:</strong> TF-IDF with unigrams + bigrams, sublinear term-frequency scaling (<code>sublinear_tf</code> added +6.0% CV accuracy), 5,000-term vocabulary.</li>
<li><strong>Dimensionality reduction:</strong> Truncated SVD (works directly on sparse matrices, no centering), k = 300 components.</li>
<li><strong>Models:</strong> K-Means and Spectral clustering (unsupervised); Logistic Regression and Linear SVM with 5-fold CV (supervised).</li>
</ul>
